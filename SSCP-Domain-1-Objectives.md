# [Domain-1](#domain-1-security-concepts-and-practices) **Security Concepts and Practices**

- **16% weighting**, tied for the heaviest domain — renamed from "Security Operations and Administration" in the 2024 refresh to better reflect its foundational, practitioner-level scope

## 1.1 Comply with codes of ethics

- The **(ISC)² Code of Ethics** applies to SSCP holders the same as it does to CISSP holders (see CISSP Domain 1.1) — protect society and the infrastructure, act honorably and legally, serve principals diligently, and advance the profession
- **Organizational code of ethics**: a distinct, additional obligation layered on top of the (ISC)² code, not a substitute for it

## 1.2 Understand security concepts

- **CIA + AA**: Confidentiality, Integrity, Availability, Accountability, and Non-repudiation — the same pillars referenced throughout the CBK (see CISSP Domain 1.2)
- **Least privilege**: granting only the minimum access needed to perform a function
- **Segregation of Duties (SoD)**: splitting sensitive tasks across roles so no single person can complete a high-risk process alone — both concepts recur constantly through Domains 2 and 7

## 1.3 Identify and implement security controls

- **Technical controls** (firewalls, IDS, ACLs), **physical controls** (mantraps, cameras, locks), and **administrative controls** (policies, standards, procedures, baselines) — the same three-category model referenced across the security management CBK
- **Assessing compliance requirements and periodic audit/review**: an SSCP is expected to help verify controls stay aligned to applicable requirements over time, not just implement them once

## 1.4 Document and maintain functional security controls

- **Control types**: deterrent, preventive, detective, corrective, compensating — the practical, day-to-day application of the same control taxonomy from Domain 1.3, now framed around ongoing documentation and maintenance rather than initial selection

## 1.5 Support and implement asset management lifecycle (hardware, software, and data)

- Full lifecycle stages: **planning/design/initiation → development/acquisition (e.g., DevSecOps, testing) → inventory and licensing (open- vs. closed-source) → implementation/assessment → operation/maintenance/End of Life (EOL) → archival/retention → disposal/destruction**
- An SSCP's role is largely operational here — executing and maintaining the asset lifecycle processes that Domain 1's CISSP-level counterparts (Domain 2) design at a policy level

## 1.6 Support and/or implement change management lifecycle

- **Change management**: defined roles, responsibilities, processes, communications, and audit trail for any change
- **Security impact analysis**: assessing a proposed change's security implications *before* approval
- **Configuration Management (CM)**: maintaining and enforcing an approved baseline configuration over time

## 1.7 Support and/or implement security awareness and training

- Practical delivery of awareness content — social engineering/phishing simulations, tabletop exercises, and ongoing awareness communications — the operational execution of the awareness program concepts covered at a higher level in CISM Domain 3.B.4 and CISSP Domain 1.12

## 1.8 Collaborate with physical security operations

- Data center/facility security assessment, badging and visitor management, and personal device restrictions — an SSCP typically works *alongside* dedicated physical security functions rather than owning them outright, contributing the IT/security administration perspective

## AI integration in this domain (2025–26 outline update)

- ISC2's current outline explicitly ties this domain to AI system governance: applying the same core security pillars to automated/AI-driven processes, verifying AI outputs remain reliable and tamper-free ("algorithmic integrity"), and extending change management practices to account for the update/versioning cycles of machine learning models specifically
